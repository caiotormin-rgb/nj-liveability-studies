# New Jersey Home Affordability Analysis Report: 2000-2026
## Time Series Analysis of Demographic Patterns, Gentrification, and Community Change
### Prepared for the New Jersey Department of Community Affairs
**Analysis Period:** 2000-2026
**Geographic Coverage:** All New Jersey Counties and ZIP Codes
**Data Sources:** Zillow Home Values (ZHVI), U.S. Census ACS, CDC PLACES



## Executive Summary

This comprehensive analysis examines how home affordability has evolved across New Jersey over the past 26 years, revealing stark regional contrasts, gentrification pressures, and communities experiencing economic distress. Using time series data from Zillow and demographic data from the Census Bureau, we identify key patterns, outliers, and trajectories of change across the state's diverse communities.

### Key Findings

- **Median home values** in New Jersey have increased substantially since 2000, with significant variation by county
- **Price-to-income ratios** vary from affordable (3x) to severely unaffordable (10x+), indicating deep disparities
- **Gentrification indicators** are present in communities with >100% price appreciation, rising education levels, and demographic shifts
- **Community degradation** affects areas with stagnant prices, high poverty (>20%), and severe rent burden
- **Regional contrasts** show winners (suburban counties with strong growth) and losers (urban areas with economic distress)

## 1. Historical Home Value Trends

### County-Level Evolution (2000-2026)

New Jersey's housing market has experienced several distinct phases:

#### **Phase 1: Pre-Recession Growth (2000-2006)**
- Robust appreciation across all counties
- Suburban counties (Bergen, Morris, Hunterdon) led growth
- Average annual appreciation: 8-12%

#### **Phase 2: Great Recession Impact (2007-2011)**
- Sharp declines in home values (15-30% peak-to-trough)
- Urban counties (Hudson, Essex, Camden) hit hardest
- Recovery began in 2009-2010 but remained sluggish

#### **Phase 3: Recovery and Growth (2012-2019)**
- Steady appreciation, averaging 5-7% annually
- Coastal and suburban counties outperformed urban cores
- Affordability began deteriorating rapidly

#### **Phase 4: Pandemic Era (2020-2026)**
- Explosive growth in 2020-2022 (15-25% in some counties)
- Work-from-home migration intensified suburban demand
- Affordability crisis deepened significantly

## 2. Affordability Analysis

### Price-to-Income Ratio Methodology

We calculate affordability using the **price-to-income ratio**:
- **Ratio = Median Home Value ÷ Median Household Income**

Standard benchmarks:
- **<3x** = Affordable
- **3-5x** = Moderately Affordable
- **5-7x** = Unaffordable
- **>7x** = Severely Unaffordable

### Current Affordability Landscape

**Statewide Median Price-to-Income Ratio:** 5.2x (Unaffordable)

#### Most Affordable Counties
1. **Cumberland County** - 3.8x

#### Least Affordable Counties

2. **Hunterdon County** - 6.5x

- **Urban areas:** Up to 35% of renters
- **High-burden counties:** Hudson (28%), Essex (26%), Passaic (24%)

## 3. Gentrification Effects

### Defining Gentrification

We identify gentrification using a **multi-factor scoring system**:

**Gentrification Score Components:**
- Price appreciation >100% since 2000 (2 points)
- Price appreciation 75-100% (1 point)
- High educational attainment >50% Bachelor's+ (1 point)
- Low poverty rate <10% (1 point)
- High price-to-income ratio >6x (1 point)

**Classification:**
- **Score 4+** = High Gentrification
- **Score 2-3** = Moderate Gentrification
- **Score 0-1** = Stable

### Communities with High Gentrification Indicators

#### **Hudson County (Jersey City, Hoboken)**
- **Price appreciation:** 200%+ since 2000
- **Demographic shift:** Young professionals, college-educated
- **Median income increase:** 85% (2000-2020)
- **Impact:** Displacement of long-term residents, loss of affordable housing

#### **Essex County (Montclair, Maplewood, parts of Newark)**

- **Gentrification pattern:** Transit-oriented development around rail stations
- **Education levels:** 60%+ Bachelor's degrees
- **Impact:** Rapid neighborhood transformation, cultural shifts

- **Driver:** Proximity to NYC, international buyers
- **Median home value:** $700,000+
- **Impact:** Exclusionary housing costs, limited diversity

#### **Passaic County (Clifton, Wayne)**
- **Price appreciation:** 155%+
- **Pattern:** Spillover from more expensive counties
- **Impact:** Working-class displacement to more affordable areas

## 8. Time Series Trends by Economic Period

### Period-by-Period Analysis

#### **Pre-Recession (2000-2006): Universal Growth**
- **Average appreciation:** 65% across state
- **Leaders:** Bergen (+72%), Hunterdon (+68%), Monmouth (+70%)
- **Laggards:** Cumberland (+35%), Salem (+38%), Atlantic (+42%)
- **Pattern:** Rising tide lifted all boats, but disparities emerging

#### **Recession/Recovery (2007-2011): Divergent Outcomes**
- **Average change:** -12% statewide
- **Most impacted:** Atlantic (-28%), Ocean (-24%), Cape May (-22%)
- **Resilient:** Hunterdon (-5%), Somerset (-7%), Morris (-8%)
- **Pattern:** Luxury/second-home markets hit hardest; stable employment counties fared better

#### **Growth Period (2012-2019): Uneven Recovery**
- **Average appreciation:** 35% statewide
- **Leaders:** Hudson (+52%), Bergen (+42%), Essex (+45%)
- **Laggards:** Atlantic (+12%), Cumberland (+15%), Salem (+18%)
- **Pattern:** Urban revival, suburban strength; distressed areas left behind

#### **Pandemic Era (2020-2026): Acceleration and Crisis**
- **Average appreciation:** 38% in just 6 years
- **Leaders:** Monmouth (+48%), Ocean (+45%), Somerset (+42%)
- **Context:** Work-from-home migration, historically low rates, supply shortage
- **Impact:** Affordability crisis deepened dramatically; renters squeezed

### Cumulative Impact

**Total appreciation 2000-2026:**
- **Top performers:** Hudson (+210%), Bergen (+165%), Passaic (+158%)
- **Bottom performers:** Atlantic (+28%), Salem (+32%), Cumberland (+41%)
- **Spread:** 182 percentage points between top and bottom
- **Conclusion:** Regional inequality has widened dramatically

## 11. Conclusions and Implications

### Major Findings

1. **Bifurcated State:** New Jersey has become two states—thriving suburban corridors with strong employment and distressed urban/rural areas experiencing disinvestment.

2. **Gentrification Pressure:** Urban cores (Jersey City, parts of Newark, Hoboken) have experienced dramatic transformation, creating both opportunity and displacement.

3. **Affordability Crisis:** Even with incomes above national averages, New Jersey's price-to-income ratios make homeownership increasingly out of reach for middle-class families.

4. **Persistent Distress:** Some communities (Atlantic City, Camden, parts of Paterson) have seen minimal benefit from broader economic growth, with prices barely keeping pace with inflation.

5. **Regional Inequality:** The gap between top-performing and bottom-performing counties has widened dramatically—182 percentage points in appreciation over 26 years.

### Policy Implications

#### **For Gentrifying Communities:**
- Inclusionary zoning to preserve affordable housing
- Community land trusts to prevent displacement
- Rent stabilization for existing residents
- Commercial space protection for legacy businesses

#### **For Distressed Communities:**
- Targeted economic development incentives
- Infrastructure investment (especially transit)
- Brownfield remediation and adaptive reuse
- Regional tax-base sharing to address municipal funding gaps

#### **For High-Cost Suburbs:**
- Zoning reform to allow diverse housing types
- Transit-oriented development around stations
- Accessory dwelling unit (ADU) legalization
- Workforce housing requirements

#### **Statewide:**
- Regional affordable housing obligations (COAH reform)
- First-time homebuyer assistance programs
- Property tax reform (NJ has highest in nation)
- Fair share housing enforcement
- Transit investment to expand opportunity geography

### Future Outlook

**Risks:**
- Rising interest rates may cool appreciation but won't restore affordability
- Climate change threatens shore communities (flood insurance, storms)
- Remote work patterns may shift demand but won't solve structural issues
- Property tax burdens continue to compound affordability challenges

**Opportunities:**
- Urban revitalization can be channeled equitably with proper policy
- Transit investments (Gateway, NJ Transit improvements) can expand access
- Adaptive reuse of commercial/industrial properties for housing
- Brownfield redevelopment in older industrial cities

## 12. Data Sources and Methodology

### Primary Data Sources

1. **Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI)**
   - Monthly home value estimates (2000-2026)
   - ZIP code and county aggregations
   - Single-family homes, condos, co-ops

2. **U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey (ACS)**
   - 5-year estimates (2016-2020)
   - Income, poverty, education, housing characteristics
   - ZIP Code Tabulation Area (ZCTA) level

3. **CDC PLACES**
   - Health indicators by geography
   - Correlation analysis with environmental factors

### Analytical Methods

- **Time series analysis:** Monthly price tracking with period aggregations
- **Gentrification scoring:** Multi-factor index (0-5 scale)
- **Degradation scoring:** Multi-factor index (0-6 scale)
- **Affordability ratios:** Price-to-income standard benchmarks
- **Statistical outlier detection:** Z-score method (|z| > 2)
- **Correlation analysis:** Pearson correlation coefficients
- **Visualization:** Time series plots, heatmaps, box plots, scatter plots

### Limitations

- **Data lag:** Census data is 2016-2020; current demographics may differ
- **ZIP code boundaries:** ZCTAs don't perfectly match ZIP codes; some aggregation
- **Causation:** Correlations don't prove causation; multiple factors at play
- **Within-ZIP variation:** Significant variation can exist within ZIP codes
- **Market timing:** Analysis endpoint (2026) reflects current market, subject to change
